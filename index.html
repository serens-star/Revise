About this design

Revise treats the interface itself as an argument. Its question is what is lost when the friction of thinking is removed, and whether a deliberately slow, human-first interface can restore a sense of authorship.

Why help is withheld. Gerlich (2025) reports a negative relationship between frequent AI use and critical thinking, linked to cognitive offloading. The tool does not refuse AI. It changes the terms, so you think first.

Why the answers are partial. Nosta (2025) describes AI output as borrowed certainty: fluent language with no visible labour behind it. The model here is told to leave gaps, ask questions, and never write for you, so the uncertainty stays yours.

Why the delay cannot be skipped. Gosline, reported by Andon (2025), argues that some friction is what makes deliberation happen. The wait, the justification and the partial answer are designed friction, not technical limits.

Why it looks like this. The Slow Web movement (Fiedel, 2015; IndieWeb, 2026) treats pacing as a value choice. Hence the whitespace, the slow transitions, and the absence of badges, streaks or urgency.

Nothing you write is stored. The only thing sent anywhere is the help request itself. Everything else stays in this browser tab and disappears when you close it.
